iPhones save photos in HEIC format by default to use less storage while keeping high quality. The problem: many Windows PCs, Android devices, and websites don’t open HEIC files. Converting HEIC to JPG fixes compatibility so you can share, upload, or edit your iPhone photos anywhere. This guide shows you how to convert HEIC to JPG for free and how to make your iPhone save JPG directly if you prefer.

## What is HEIC format?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is Apple’s default image format on iPhones and iPads since iOS 11. It uses HEVC (H.265) compression and typically produces files 50% smaller than JPG at similar quality. HEIC also supports multiple images in one file (e.g. Live Photos) and 10‑bit color. The downside is that outside the Apple ecosystem, support is still limited: many apps and operating systems only recognise JPG and PNG.

## Why convert HEIC to JPG?

- **Windows** — Older Windows versions don’t open HEIC by default; even with the Microsoft extension, some apps and workflows expect JPG.
- **Android** — Most Android devices and apps don’t handle HEIC; sharing with Android users is easier with JPG.
- **Social media and websites** — Upload forms and platforms often accept only JPG (and PNG). Converting to JPG avoids “unsupported format” errors.
- **Print and professional use** — Many print shops and design tools work with JPG; HEIC can cause delays or refusals.
- **Email and attachments** — Recipients on older systems may not be able to open HEIC; JPG is the universal fallback.

Converting to JPG keeps quality high while making your photos usable everywhere.

## How to change iPhone camera to JPG directly

If you’d rather have new photos saved as JPG on your iPhone so you don’t need to convert later:

1. Open **Settings** on your iPhone.
2. Go to **Camera** (or **Photos** on some iOS versions).
3. Tap **Formats**.
4. Select **Most Compatible** instead of **High Efficiency**.

With “Most Compatible” enabled, new photos and videos are saved as JPG and MP4 instead of HEIC and HEVC. Existing HEIC photos stay as HEIC until you convert them. This uses more storage but guarantees compatibility with any device or service.

## HEIC vs JPG: which is better?

- **HEIC** — Smaller files, better for storage on the iPhone, supports 10‑bit and Live Photos. Best when you stay in the Apple ecosystem.
- **JPG** — Works everywhere: Windows, Android, web, email, print. Slightly larger files at similar quality. Best when you share across devices and platforms.

So: use **HEIC** to save space on your iPhone; convert to **JPG** when you need to share, upload, or use the photos on non‑Apple devices.
