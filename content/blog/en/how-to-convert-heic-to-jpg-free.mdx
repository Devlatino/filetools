## What Is HEIC and Why Does iPhone Use It?

HEIC stands for High Efficiency Image Container. Apple introduced it in iOS 11 as the default camera format. The reason is simple: HEIC files are roughly half the size of JPG files at the same visual quality. This means more photos fit on your iPhone without filling up your storage.

The problem is compatibility. HEIC is an Apple format and while modern Macs open it natively, Windows 10 and 11 require a paid codec from the Microsoft Store to view HEIC files. Android phones don't support it at all. Most social media platforms, web forms, and document editors expect JPG or PNG — not HEIC.

- **Uploading to websites and web forms** that only accept JPG or PNG
- **Editing photos in software** like Photoshop, Lightroom, or GIMP
- **Sharing photos on social media** platforms that don't accept HEIC
- **Printing photos** at a photo lab or print service
- **Attaching photos to documents** like CVs or application forms

## How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 10 and 11

If you want to convert HEIC files without opening a browser, Windows offers a few built-in options:

**Option 1: Use the Photos app (requires codec)**
Microsoft offers the HEIF Image Extensions codec on the Microsoft Store. Once installed, the Photos app can open and save HEIC files as JPG. However, this codec costs $0.99 in some regions.

**Option 2: Use Paint**
In newer Windows 11 builds, you can open HEIC files directly in Paint after installing the codec, then File → Save As → JPEG.

## How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Mac

On macOS, HEIC files open automatically in Preview. To convert to JPG:

1. Open the HEIC file in Preview
2. Go to File → Export
3. Select JPEG from the format dropdown
4. Adjust quality if needed and click Save

## How to Stop iPhone from Taking HEIC Photos

If you prefer your iPhone to always shoot in JPG format, you can change the camera settings:

1. Open **Settings** on your iPhone
2. Scroll down to **Camera**
3. Tap **Formats**
4. Select **Most Compatible** instead of High Efficiency

This tells the camera to save photos as JPG. Note that JPG photos will use more storage space than HEIC.
